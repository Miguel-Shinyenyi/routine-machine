# Routine Machine: product direction, October 2026

Written 2026-10-18. It records the direction the founder set after Phases 5 to 8. Sections
marked "decided" are the founder's calls. Sections marked "proposed" are recommendations
to test. The design for every screen named here is in `docs/design-reference/`.

## Why it exists (decided)

- **Ultimate aim:** find ways to address hype culture. Hype culture is the strong, partly
  imagined order of constant competition that grows out of a human instinct to compare and
  compete.
- **Current goal:** help people return to meaningful work after they fall out of routine.
  Persuade with reasons, including the philosophical articles the founder writes, never
  with pressure.
- **The first customer is the founder.** Hype culture gets studied before anyone tries to
  "fix" it. Changing a social order, imagined or not, has consequences. The aim is for
  this product's consequences to be more positive than negative, and that has to be
  measured, not assumed.

## Who it is for, in order (decided)

1. **Individual developers.** Developers are always learning, and they live inside hype
   cycles: crypto in 2021 and 2022, AI now. Each person tracks their own learning and
   routines.
2. **Organisations that train developers.** Bootcamps and training programmes, such as
   Moringa School, buy seats in bulk. Each learner tracks their own progress. The
   programme sees cohort totals only.
3. **Everyone else, later.** Other people come in after the developer version proves
   itself.

## New capabilities

### 1. Profile and interests (decided; design: `profile.html`)
- **What it holds:** the user's interests (football, music, cooking and so on), the
  thinkers they return to, how they like to learn, time zone and currency.
- **Analogies:** explanations of learning topics use those interests. For example,
  idempotency is explained in football terms: a goal counts once, however many replays
  are shown.
- **Always the precise version too.** The analogy comes first, then the precise
  definition. A switch shows only the plain version.
- **Private.** Interests never reach the hillside or a programme admin.
- **Proposed:** the analogy is generated by the intelligence service with the topic, one
  chosen interest and a rubric. It is cached per topic and interest, and labelled as an
  analogy, never as a source of truth.

### 2. The granary: financial discipline (decided; design: `granary.html`, `phone-granary.html`)
- **Why:** hype culture costs money as well as time. Examples are crypto presales,
  betting tips, courses bought on impulse and digital overdrafts.
- **Small savings:** a daily amount the user picks (KSh 50 in the design). It's counted
  in days, like everything else, and a missed day takes nothing out.
- **The pause:** before a tempting payment, the user writes what it is, how much, why now
  and where they heard it. Routine Machine asks again after 24 hours and once more after
  30 days, so the user learns from their own record.
- **Spending categories:** imported from the user's M-Pesa statement, the PDF they
  download from their M-Pesa app. Only category totals are kept, and the file is deleted
  after parsing. Bank CSV and money market fund statements come next.
- **Integrate, don't rebuild (proposed):** Routine Machine never holds or moves money and
  never gives investment advice. Savings sit in the user's own products (a money market
  fund such as Safaricom's Ziidi, a bank or a SACCO). Routine Machine records that a
  saving happened. Any API partnership comes only when a partner offers one and a pilot
  needs it.
- **Private by rule.** Money never reaches the hillside or a programme.

### 3. Organisations and cohorts (decided; design: `cohort.html`)
- **Running a cohort:**
  - Bulk seats and invites by CSV.
  - A cohort dashboard showing the pilot measures from the operating plan:
    - activation within 7 days;
    - weekly participation;
    - return within 7 days after a missed day;
    - the optional "supportive, neutral or pressuring" check;
    - each figure with its denominator.
- **Small groups stay hidden.** Groups under 5 people are not shown.
- **Admins see aggregates only.** Learners can read the same page the admin sees.

### 4. Hillside v2 (decided from the leaderboard design notes; design: `hillside.html`)
- **Who's on it:** it's opt-in and limited to an invited cohort by default. An open
  hillside is a second, optional choice. Users can hide it at any time, which gives a
  personal-only view.
- **Seasons:** weekly. The board starts fresh every Monday, and there is no all-time
  ranking.
- **Credits:**
  - One credit per qualifying goal per local calendar day, with a daily cap (3 in the
    design).
  - Extra evidence may raise confidence, but never multiplies credit.
  - A missed day subtracts nothing.
- **Returns:** coming back after a missed day is counted separately and shown as a
  cohort total. It never ranks anyone.
- **Transparency:** a credit ledger shows each piece of evidence, its status (accepted,
  self-reported, pending, not counted), the credit and the versioned rule that decided it.
  Users can question a credit.
- **No "discipline score".** A record of behaviour is not a verdict on a person.

## The evidence-to-credit pipeline (proposed, from the leaderboard design notes)

1. **Ingest:** the event, its source, timestamp, goal and minimal metadata.
2. **Validate:** schema, permissions, source authenticity where possible, and time
   boundaries.
3. **Normalise:** convert to one evidence record and keep the event's origin.
4. **Deduplicate:** stable source event ids and idempotency keys, with database
   uniqueness enforcing them.
5. **Qualify:** apply the goal's explicit rule. Label the evidence accepted, rejected,
   pending or self-reported.
6. **Aggregate:** apply the per-goal, per-day cap, with
   `UNIQUE (user_id, goal_id, local_date)` on the daily-credit table.
7. **Publish:** the season score is derived from accepted credits. Only aggregates are
   exposed.
8. **Audit and correct:** store the rule version with every decision. Scores can be
   recomputed after a bug fix. A cached score is never the record of truth.

**Time:**
- Timestamps are stored in UTC.
- The day is decided in the user's configured time zone, and the zone used is recorded
  on each credit.
- A time-zone change is not treated as suspicious.

## Principle that runs through all of it

Evidence of activity is not evidence of quality, understanding or outcome.
- WakaTime proves a coding session was recorded, not that the code works.
- An AI review of a reading note is an assessment against criteria, not proof of
  understanding.

The product says this plainly wherever it shows a number.
